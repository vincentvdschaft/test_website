IQ demodulation

IQ demodulation is a method to decompose a signal that is modulated with a carrier frequency ω\omegaω into a signal I(t)I(t)I(t), that is in phase with the carrier cos⁡(ωt)\cos(\omega t)cos(ωt), and a signal Q(t)Q(t)Q(t), that has a 90∘90^\circ90∘ phase shift:

x(t)=I(t)⋅cos⁡(ωt)−Q(t)⋅sin⁡(ωt)(1)x(t)=I(t)\cdot\cos(\omega t) - Q(t)\cdot\sin(\omega t) \tag{1}x(t)=I(t)⋅cos(ωt)−Q(t)⋅sin(ωt)(1)

Complex representation

The in-phase, and quadrature components are often represented as a single complex number:

IQ(t)=I(t)+i⋅Q(t)IQ(t) = I(t) + i\cdot Q(t)IQ(t)=I(t)+i⋅Q(t)

[!info] Note Note the sign difference between the two!

Performing I/Q demodulation

The most common way to perform I/Q demodulation on sampled data with carrier frequency ωc=2πfc\omega_c=2\pi f_cωc​=2πfc​ is as follows:

  1. Compute the analytic signal using the Hilbert transform. The analytic signal is just the signal without the negative frequency components. This means that the analytic signal is complex-valued.
  2. Shift the one-sided spectrum to baseband by multiplying with a signal e−i2πfcte^{-i2\pi f_c t}e−i2πfc​t.
  3. Low-pass filter to remove noise from outside the bandwidth of the signal.
  4. If we then want to perform envelope detection this is as simple as taking the magnitude of the complex valued I/Q signal.
